01

The finding

A systematic search for tradeable cross-sectional alpha in liquid US equities, conducted under a cash-account constraint, produced no signal. That outcome is unsurprising. What makes it worth writing down is that the search also produced a quantified explanation for why, and a calibration procedure that turned every subsequent null from a disappointment into a prediction.

The gap between what this universe offers and what a long-only book requires is arithmetic, not luck — and no amount of additional price-derived signal can close it.

Three numbers carry the argument. A synthetic forecast injected through the actual risk stack needs an information coefficient near 0.036 to beat holding everything. The best stable feature available from free price data measures 0.012. And because those features correlate with one another at ρ = 0.234, combining arbitrarily many of them converges to 0.0223 — roughly 60% of the requirement, approached asymptotically and never crossed.

The remainder of this document covers the methodology that made those numbers trustworthy, four claims that were retracted along the way, and the two strategies that did survive scrutiny — neither of which is alpha.

02

The control that changed everything

Roughly a dozen families were tested and deflated individually before a basic question got asked: what does a signal containing no information score on these same tests?

The answer invalidated every prior conclusion. Forty random signals, routed through the identical construction — same universe, same top-30% selection, same inverse-volatility weighting, same 10% volatility target, same costs — produced this:

A signal with no information whatsoever clears the conventional |t| > 2 bar comfortably, and reaches the Harvey-Liu-Zhu |t| > 3 threshold about one time in twenty.

The mechanism is the volatility-targeting overlay. It cuts market exposure when trailing volatility rises — which is when the benchmark crashes — giving the book a time-varying beta that a constant-beta factor model cannot represent. The unmodelled timing surfaces as alpha. Ferson & Schadt described this in 1996; the diagnosis was reached here by accident, thirty years late.

The third row is the confirmation: moving the sample start from 2005 to 2015 collapses the phantom alpha from t = 2.75 to 0.77. Remove the 2008 crash and there is nothing left to dodge.

A t-statistic of 6 is not evidence of a business. The signal composite reached exactly that — and still lost to holding everything.

The no-signal book ranks first of thirty-eight. Two strategies cleared the unconditional null against a chance expectation of 1.9; neither survived once beta was allowed to vary. R² against Fama-French runs 0.66–0.75 for most rows — two thirds to three quarters of everything built here is factor exposure purchasable in an ETF.

Notable individual outcomes. Post-earnings announcement drift, the most replicated anomaly in finance, produced a 60-day decile spread of 0.06% against a documented 3–6% — not a weakened effect but an absent one. Book-to-market was the strongest single measurement in the project at IC 0.0448, and still landed at the 85th percentile. And the 7-signal composite ranked below its own components, because averaging z-scores of signals correlated at 0.97 averages one signal with itself and adds noise from the rest.

Non-linear methods

That composite failure is direct evidence the combination method was destroying information, which makes non-linear models the best-motivated variant to test. Gu, Kelly & Xiu report roughly a doubling of out-of-sample R² over linear methods on comparable characteristic sets. Walk-forward across 35 features, training on everything before year Y−1 and predicting Y:

Trees beat linear by 29%, not the 2× reported. Note that ridge finished last, below equal-weighting — so the story is not "fitting beats not fitting"; ridge overfits and generalises poorly while trees regularise.

The random forest was the only model to survive a full stress battery: 18 of 18 hyperparameter configurations positive with a tight spread, seed-to-seed variation of 5%, feature-importance rank correlation of 0.864 year over year, and a label-shuffle null topping out at IC 0.0045 against its 0.0308. It is genuinely finding structure.

What it found was a regime-specific inversion of the low-volatility anomaly — a relationship that held over one sample and has the opposite sign in the documented literature.

Its top features are all volatility and liquidity measures, and its predictions correlate −0.472 with low-volatility: it bets on high volatility, which won over 2009–2026 and is the reverse of the documented effect. That is a well-fitted description of one regime rather than an edge, and it still produces a worse portfolio than holding everything.

04

Retractions

Four conclusions were stated with confidence and later overturned by a control that should have run first. They are recorded because the pattern — announcing a finding at the point it became interesting rather than the point it became controlled — is the most transferable lesson here.

Claimed
A pairwise relative-value signal survives every structural check — monotone gate response, sector-neutral, coherent horizon profile, t = 2.45.
Revised
Extending the sample from 7 to 20 years dropped t to 1.28 where a real effect predicts 4.1. Sample-specific artifact; every supporting result reversed.
Claimed
A genuine residual of +0.02 to +0.036 Sharpe survives the beta-matched control.
Revised
That range is the noise floor. Random signals score +0.029 on the same control, which cannot represent a time-varying beta.
Claimed
Small caps fail because wider spreads cancel their larger premia — a 12× cost penalty.
Revised
The 12× figure was invented. Corwin-Schultz and Abdi-Ranaldo measure 2.0×. Small caps still fail — from concentration cost in a high-idiosyncratic-vol universe, and a benchmark at Sharpe 0.11 not worth beating.
Claimed
A turnover bug charged the benchmark nothing, tilting every comparison in this project.
Revised
Real bug, measured impact 5.8bp/yr and 0.003 Sharpe. Immaterial. The claim preceded the measurement.

Two code defects were also found and fixed: Sortino ran 16% high (standard deviation of the negative subset rather than the RMS of the clipped series), and turnover was charged on target changes rather than realised drift. Sharpe, drawdown, volatility and CAGR all verified against empyrical.

05

What actually cleared

Only after twelve families had failed was the correct literature consulted. The anomaly literature catalogues effects that get arbitraged away. The risk premia literature catalogues compensation for bearing risk somebody else wants shed — which persists, because publication does not remove the underlying demand.

Two results came from that layer, and both are diversifiers rather than edges.

Long/short trend is the only result in the project to land beyond the maximum of 40 null draws. The long-only version, which is what a cash account permits, sits at the 48th percentile on standalone Sharpe — the null median.

Volatility risk premium
Implied volatility exceeded subsequent realised volatility 84% of the time across four distinct eras (4.85 / 3.34 / 3.16 / 3.77 pp, 1996–2026) with no downward trend. Unlike every anomaly tested, it has not decayed. But CBOE's own benchmark indices underperform buy-and-hold (PUT 8.48% vs SPX total return ~10.25%) and carry negative alpha beside a 60/40 core. A risk transformation, not free money.
Time-series trend
The only strategy with negative correlation to the core (−0.15), making money in two of three crises — +6.2% through the GFC while SPY lost 55%. Fama-French alpha is 0.26% at t = 0.26, so the entire case rests on the correlation, not on outperformance. And the version that clears requires a margin account.
06

Binding constraints

Free data was never what limited this. Four things were, and each was measured rather than assumed.

Universe
Liquid US large caps are the most arbitraged equity segment in existence. Chordia, Subrahmanyam & Tong measured anomaly returns roughly halving after decimalisation. Small caps are not an escape: correcting the cost assumption made them worse, because concentration in a high-idiosyncratic-vol universe loses more to diversification than any forecast recovers.
Long-only
Costs roughly half the achievable information ratio (Clarke, de Silva & Thorley). Applied to trend, it removes precisely the crisis alpha the strategy is held for.
Construction
Truncating a ranking at the top 30% was the worst sizing rule tested — discarding magnitude and two thirds of the diversification at once. It was nonetheless the default for every test in this project until the last day.
Capital
Data costs are fixed; returns scale with capital. A $150/month subscription adding a genuine 2% annually breaks even at $90,000 invested. Below that threshold, paid data buys precision in losing money.
07

What the work produced

No tradeable signal. Two things of durable value instead.

A calibration procedure. Run zero-information signals through the identical pipeline and report a percentile. Every overlay — volatility targeting, inverse-vol weighting, truncation, band rebalancing — must sit inside that control, because each changes the return distribution independently of the signal. Conventional thresholds quoted without this calibration are meaningless: on this stack, noise reaches t = 2.75.

Infrastructure that is correct. A backtest engine tracking true holdings drift, metrics verified against a reference implementation, EDGAR retrieval with the pagination and primary-document traps solved, 13F and fundamentals ingestion, an options collector running daily, and 35 regression tests pinning the specific bugs found. One test constructs a zero-information volatility-timing sleeve and asserts it still beats a naive control — pinning the exact illusion that produced two retracted conclusions.

The literature cannot give you signals. It can stop you believing in ones you do not have — which, on this evidence, is the more valuable of the two.
