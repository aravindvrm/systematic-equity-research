A Measured Ceiling

A strategy containing no information scores a Fama-French alpha t of 2.75 on this pipeline — clearing every significance bar in the literature while forecasting nothing. Twelve signal families and thirty-eight strategies, searched from a retail cash account, measured against that floor rather than against convention. The same procedure later overturned this paper's own account of why the floor exists.

The finding

The question here was practical rather than academic: whether a small retail cash account, already traded profitably by hand, could hand any part of that work to a systematic strategy. Not a fund's strategy — one reachable from where a retail trader actually stands.

That position is specific, and every part of it binds. A cash account means no shorting and no leverage, so every book must be long-only. Free data means SEC filings, Yahoo prices and the Ken French library — no fundamentals vendor, no tick data, no alternative sets. Liquid US large caps are what a small account can trade without moving the price, and they are also the most heavily arbitraged segment in existence. And small capital means fixed costs are not amortised away: a $150/month data subscription is a different proposition at $30,000 than at $30 million.

The honest prior was that this probably fails — Barber & Odean measured retail accounts underperforming the market after costs, and Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti found almost no day traders profitable over any sustained period. What made it worth doing anyway is that "probably fails" is not a number, and the failure mode of a retail systematic strategy is not obviously the same as a fund's — different constraints could plausibly leave different things on the table.

They do not, and the search produced no signal. That outcome is unsurprising. What makes it worth writing down is that the search also produced a quantified explanation for why, and a calibration procedure that turned every subsequent null from a disappointment into a prediction.

The gap between what this universe offers and what a long-only book requires is arithmetic, not luck — and no amount of additional price-derived signal can close it.

Book
The set of positions actually held. A long-only book can buy a stock or not hold it, but cannot short it, so its only way to express a negative view is to own none.
No-signal book
The benchmark used throughout. It holds the entire universe — no ranking, no selection, no forecast — and is run through the identical construction as every strategy tested: inverse-volatility weights, a 10% volatility target, monthly rebalancing, the same costs. The volatility target only ever cuts exposure — the scaling factor is capped at 1.0, so the book never borrows and stays implementable in a cash account. It is what a signal has to beat to be worth having, and all thirty-eight lost to it.
Information coefficient
The cross-sectional correlation between a forecast and the return that follows it — skill per bet, on a −1 to +1 scale. Because it is a correlation, its square is the share of return variance the forecast explains, which is what makes the scale so unintuitive: the 0.036 required here accounts for 0.13% of the cross-sectional variance in what happens next, and the 0.012 available accounts for 0.014%. Almost all of the variation is unforecastable, and the entire question is whether the sliver that is not covers its own costs. The three headline figures are all on this scale.
Cash account
A retail brokerage account with no margin: no shorting, no leverage, settled funds only. It is the constraint this study was conducted under, and it is binding — it forces every book to be long-only, and it rules out the one strategy that ultimately cleared the null.

The organising framework is the Fundamental Law of Active Management (Grinold, 1989), extended for real-world constraints by Clarke, de Silva & Thorley (2002):

IR  ≈  IC  ×  √BR  ×  TC

Skill per bet (IC), independent bets per year (BR), and the transfer coefficient — the fraction of a forecast that survives the constraints under which it is actually implemented. A long-only book runs a TC of roughly 0.5–0.6, because it can overweight without limit but can only underweight to zero. Half the achievable information ratio is forfeited before any signal question is asked.

The threshold used throughout this study is not derived from that identity, however. It is measured: synthetic forecasts of known IC were injected through the actual risk stack — the real universe, weighting, volatility target, costs and rebalance schedule — and the break-even read off directly. That captures the true transfer coefficient of this specific implementation rather than assuming a textbook value. The Law explains the result; it does not produce it.

Three numbers follow. Beating a no-signal book requires an information coefficient near 0.036. The best stable feature available from free price data measures 0.012, and the eight tested average 0.0108. Because those features correlate with one another at ρ = 0.234, combining arbitrarily many of them converges on 0.0108 / √0.234 = 0.0223 — roughly 60% of the requirement, approached asymptotically and never crossed.

The asymptote is computed from the mean individual IC across the eight signals, not the best one, since it describes what a large combination of comparable signals converges to rather than what the strongest single signal achieves. It also assumes the idealised equicorrelation case — equally skilled signals sharing one pairwise correlation. That is a simplification of the measured covariance structure, so read 0.0223 as the asymptote implied by the average signal and the average correlation, not as a bound derived from the full covariance matrix.

Eight price signals correlate at ρ = 0.234, so they contribute an effective breadth of 3.03 rather than 8. At their mean individual IC of 0.0108, adding arbitrarily many more converges to 0.0108 / √0.234 = 0.0223 — the gap to 0.036 does not close from within this feature space.

What this does and does not show. The reachable set from the position above is empty, and the arithmetic says why. It does not show that markets are efficient, that cross-sectional signal does not exist, or that the funds harvesting it are wrong. Relaxing the cash constraint was tested rather than assumed, twice: a dollar-neutral long/short book on the strongest signal, which failed, and long/short trend, which is the one result in the project that cleared and is reported in section 05. Paid data, a wider or less liquid universe, and shorter holding periods are genuinely untested, and each is a live route these constraints foreclose. The result is a boundary on one position, not a claim about the market.

The remainder of this document covers the methodology that made those numbers trustworthy, five conclusions the controls overturned — including one of this paper's own headline claims — and the two strategies that did survive scrutiny — neither of which is alpha.

02

The control that changed everything

Roughly a dozen families were tested and deflated individually before a basic question got asked: what does a signal containing no information score on these same tests?

The answer invalidated every prior conclusion. Forty random signals, routed through the identical construction — same universe, same top-30% selection, same inverse-volatility weighting, same 10% volatility target capped at 1.0× exposure, same costs — produced this:

A signal with no information whatsoever clears the conventional |t| > 2 bar comfortably, and reaches the Harvey-Liu-Zhu |t| > 3 threshold about one time in twenty.

Neither book forecasts anything — both hold a randomly chosen 30% of the universe. The only difference is the volatility-targeting overlay, which cuts exposure as volatility rises and so avoids roughly half the 2008 loss. A constant-beta factor model cannot represent that timing, and reads the difference as alpha. That much is real, and it was this project's explanation for the floor for most of its life. The ablation below shows it is not the right one. Generated by research/make_figures.py.

The obvious explanation is the volatility-targeting overlay. It cuts market exposure when trailing volatility rises — which is when the benchmark crashes — giving the book a time-varying beta that a constant-beta factor model cannot represent, so the unmodelled timing surfaces as alpha. Ferson & Schadt described exactly this in 1996. The effect is real and it is present here: the overlay takes the book's market beta from 0.90 to 0.48. For most of this project's life it was recorded as the cause of the floor.

Testing it refutes it. Running the identical stack twice and changing only whether the overlay is applied — same universe, same ranking, same truncation, same weighting, same schedule, same costs — with two hundred fresh null draws for each arm:

Removing the overlay raises the floor. Whatever manufactures the phantom alpha is not the overlay; the overlay slightly suppresses it. Every signal tested fails against its own arm's null in both arms, so the negative result does not depend on the overlay either — but the mechanism was wrong. (research/voltarget_ablation.py)

The universe is the index membership of today, carried backward to 2005, and that biases it twice. The companies that failed and left the index are not in it — and the companies that were added during the window are in it for the run-up that earned them their place, which they had not yet earned at the start of the sample. So a book holding everything and forecasting nothing beats a six-factor model by four and a half points a year at t = 5.79, while the actual market over the same window scores −0.40% at t = −1.77. That gap is survivorship bias, measured rather than listed as a caveat.

This is not a new result either, and the prior work is pointed. Brown, Goetzmann, Ibbotson & Ross showed in 1992 that a sample truncated by survivorship produces the appearance of predictability, and that the effect can be large enough on its own to account for the evidence then being offered for return predictability. That is this section's finding, published thirty-four years earlier. Their mechanism runs through the relationship between volatility and return in a truncated sample, which is also where the random forest in section 03 ends up — a volatility tilt that reverses the documented low-volatility effect. Two of this project's results, arrived at separately, are both downstream of one 1992 paper. Any long book drawn from this universe inherits it in proportion to its market exposure — which is precisely why volatility targeting lowers the floor rather than raising it. It cuts beta from 0.90 to 0.48, and takes part of the inherited bias out with it. Both the unconditional and conditional models live in algo/factors.py; the null floor itself is algo/evaluation.py.

The third row of the first table now reads differently too. Moving the sample start from 2005 to 2015 collapses the floor from t = 2.75 to 0.77. The original reading was that this removes the 2008 crash, leaving the overlay nothing to dodge. The simpler reading is that a decade of survivor drift compounds into less than two decades of it.

Removing the market exposure entirely settles it. Rebuilding the same stack as a long/short book whose ranking is orthogonalised to beta — realised beta 0.008, confirmed rather than assumed — and recalibrating against 200 fresh draws:

The floor tracks market exposure, not the overlay and not the signal. A book with no net exposure to this universe inherits none of its survivorship, and its floor falls below zero. A fully-exposed book inherits all of it. The overlay sits in between, which is why it moves the number a little and in the direction opposite to the one this paper claimed for most of its life. It also means the t > 2 and t > 3 conventions are not merely uncalibrated but uncalibrated by a margin that depends on the construction — the same nominal bar is trivial on one stack and unreachable on another.

A t-statistic of 6 is not evidence of a business. The signal composite reached exactly that — and still lost to holding everything.

Not one of the thirty-eight signal strategies clears the conditional null floor. Two cleared it unconditionally against a chance expectation of 1.9, and neither survived once beta was allowed to vary. The no-signal book — scored against its own null, since it holds the whole universe rather than a selection — reaches the 97.5th percentile against a best-signal 92.5th, so it outscores every one of them. R² against Fama-French runs 0.66–0.75 for most rows — two thirds to three quarters of everything built here is factor exposure purchasable in an ETF.

What this null is calibrated against. Each strategy is scored against random signals routed through its own construction, so the control covers pipeline-induced alpha: the weighting, the volatility overlay, the truncation, the costs. It does not cover the search that produced the thirty-eight. The crude family-wise check is the chance expectation above — 5% of 38 is 1.9, and two cleared unconditionally — but a proper research-process null would randomise, re-run the entire battery, take the best result, and repeat until the distribution of that maximum is known (White, 2000; Hansen, 2005). Novy-Marx (2015) did exactly this for multi-signal strategies and found the appropriate critical values run to several times the conventional ones — the closest published relative of the floor measured here, reached from the selection side rather than the construction side. That has not been run here, and it is the outstanding control on this document. Its absence cuts in a known direction: a family-wise correction raises the bar, so it can only reduce the count of strategies clearing it, never raise it. The 0 / 38 stands; what it would tighten is the reading of the two near-misses.

Notable individual outcomes. Post-earnings announcement drift, the most replicated anomaly in finance, produced a 60-day decile spread — the return gap between the top and bottom tenth of stocks ranked on it — of 0.06% against the 4.2% Bernard & Thomas (1989) report over that same sixty-day window — not a weakened effect but an absent one. Book-to-market was the strongest single measurement in the project at IC 0.0448, and still landed at the 85th percentile. And the 7-signal composite ranked below its own components. Averaging z-scores of signals correlated at 0.97 averages one signal with itself while adding the others' noise — and in the terms above, correlated signals contribute no independent bets. Measured directly: eight signals with a mean pairwise IC correlation of 0.234 amount to an effective breadth of 3.03, not eight. The denominator of √BR barely moves while the noise floor rises, so the blend lands below its own inputs.

Non-linear methods

That composite failure is direct evidence the combination method was destroying information, which makes non-linear models the best-motivated variant to test. Gu, Kelly & Xiu report roughly a doubling of out-of-sample R² over linear methods on comparable characteristic sets. Walk-forward across 35 features, training on everything before year Y−1 and predicting Y:

Trees beat linear by 29%, not the 2× reported. Note that ridge finished last, below equal-weighting — so the story is not "fitting beats not fitting"; ridge overfits and generalises poorly while trees regularise.

The random forest was the only model to survive a full stress battery: 18 of 18 hyperparameter configurations positive with a tight spread, seed-to-seed variation of 5%, feature-importance rank correlation of 0.864 year over year, and a label-shuffle null topping out at IC 0.0045 against its 0.0308. That is reproducible structure within the sample — not noise, and not yet evidence of anything persistent.

What it found was a regime-specific inversion of the low-volatility anomaly — a relationship that held over one sample and has the opposite sign in the documented literature.

Its top features are all volatility and liquidity measures, and its predictions correlate −0.472 with low-volatility: it bets on high volatility, which won over 2009–2026 and is the reverse of the documented effect. The relationship it learned is non-stationary: it held over one monetary regime and carries the opposite sign in the long-run literature, so nothing in the training data tells the model which regime it is in. A flexible learner will fit whichever sign the sample happens to contain. That is a well-specified description of 2009–2026 rather than an edge, and it still produces a worse portfolio than holding everything.

Removing the tilt confirms this directly. Orthogonalising the model's scores to beta cross-sectionally before ranking — the practical form of the residual construction in Blitz, Huij & Martens — leaves a book that earns +3.16% a year over 2015–2020 and −1.23% over 2021–2026. The residual changes sign on extension. What the forest found was the tilt.

04

What the controls overturned

Five conclusions were reached and held with confidence during the work, then overturned by a control that should have run first. Four never left the notebook — the calibration caught them before this document existed, which is what the calibration is for. The fifth did not: it was published here, as the stated mechanism behind this paper's central result, and stood for weeks before an ablation a reviewer suggested refuted it. They are recorded because the pattern that produced them — treating a finding as settled at the point it became interesting rather than the point it became controlled — is the most transferable lesson here, and because the fifth one shows the pattern surviving every control this project had until a new one was built.

Claimed
A pairwise relative-value signal survives every structural check — monotone gate response, sector-neutral, coherent horizon profile, t = 2.45.
Revised
Extending the sample from 7 to 20 years dropped t to 1.28 where a real effect predicts 4.1. Sample-specific artifact; every supporting result reversed.
Claimed
A genuine residual of +0.02 to +0.036 Sharpe survives the beta-matched control — a comparison against a passive book levered to the same average market exposure, which isolates what a strategy adds beyond simply taking more or less market risk.
Revised
That range is the noise floor. Random signals score +0.029 on the same control, which cannot represent a time-varying beta.
Claimed
Small caps fail because wider spreads cancel their larger premia — a 12× cost penalty.
Revised
The 12× figure was invented. Corwin-Schultz and Abdi-Ranaldo measure 2.0×. Small caps still fail — from concentration cost in a high-idiosyncratic-vol universe, and a benchmark at Sharpe 0.11 not worth beating.
Claimed
A turnover bug charged the benchmark nothing, tilting every comparison in this project.
Revised
Real bug, measured impact 5.8bp/yr and 0.003 Sharpe. Immaterial. The claim preceded the measurement.
Claimed
Volatility targeting is the entire source of the t = 2.75 null floor — it induces a time-varying beta that a constant-beta model books as alpha.
Revised
Backwards. Ablating the overlay raises the floor, from +2.66 to +2.94. The floor comes from survivorship in the universe, which every long book inherits in proportion to its market exposure; the overlay cuts beta from 0.90 to 0.48 and so removes part of it. The Ferson-Schadt effect is real and present, but second-order and pointing the other way. Section 02 carried the wrong mechanism for most of this project's life.

Two code defects were also found and fixed: Sortino ran 16% high (standard deviation of the negative subset rather than the RMS of the clipped series), and turnover was charged on target changes rather than realised drift. Sharpe, drawdown, volatility and CAGR all verified against empyrical.

The follow-up work described in section 06 was therefore pre-registered: the design, five numbered predictions and an explicit falsifier were written and committed before the code existed. Two of the five predictions were wrong, and the falsifier fired on a literal reading — the neutral book did clear its recalibrated null on the full sample. Two arguments for dismissing that were tried and both failed on inspection, and what actually settled it was splitting the sample, which the registered criterion had never required. Registering predictions in advance did not prevent a bad criterion. It did make the badness visible instead of negotiable.

05

What actually cleared

Only after twelve families had failed was the correct literature consulted. The anomaly literature catalogues effects that get arbitraged away. The risk premia literature catalogues compensation for bearing risk somebody else wants shed — which persists, because publication does not remove the underlying demand.

Two results came from that layer, and both are diversifiers rather than edges.

Each bar is the change in Sharpe ratio from blending a 20% sleeve of the strategy into a conventional 60% equity / 40% bond portfolio — what the strategy adds to a portfolio someone already holds, rather than how it performs alone. The null distribution is the same measurement made on 40 random-sign strategies. Long/short trend is the only result in the project to land beyond the maximum of those 40 null draws. The long-only version, which is what a cash account permits, sits at the 48th percentile on standalone Sharpe — the null median.

Volatility risk premium
Implied volatility exceeded subsequent realised volatility 84% of the time across four distinct eras (4.85 / 3.34 / 3.16 / 3.77 pp, 1996–2026) with no downward trend. Unlike every anomaly tested, it has not decayed. But CBOE's own benchmark indices underperform buy-and-hold (PUT 8.48% vs SPX total return ~10.25%) and carry negative alpha beside a 60/40 core. A risk transformation, not free money.
Time-series trend
The only strategy with negative correlation to the core (−0.15), making money in two of three crises — +6.2% through the GFC while SPY lost 55%. Fama-French alpha is 0.26% at t = 0.26, so the entire case rests on the correlation, not on outperformance. And the version that clears requires a margin account.
06

Binding constraints

Free data was never what limited this. Four things were, and each was measured rather than assumed.

Universe
Liquid US large caps are the most arbitraged equity segment in existence. Chordia, Subrahmanyam & Tong measured anomaly returns roughly halving after decimalisation, and McLean & Pontiff found published predictors decaying by a further third or so once the paper describing them is in print. Small caps are not an escape: correcting the cost assumption made them worse, because concentration in a high-idiosyncratic-vol universe loses more to diversification than any forecast recovers.
Long-only
Costs roughly half the achievable information ratio (Clarke, de Silva & Thorley). Applied to trend, it removes precisely the crisis alpha the strategy is held for. For the cross-section, though, it is not the binding constraint, and this was tested rather than argued. Three long/short constructions on the strongest signal, each carrying 50bp general-collateral borrow, no rebate on short proceeds, margin interest and a Reg T cap — the conservative retail case — and each scored against its own recalibrated 200-draw null: dollar-neutral reached Sharpe 0.735 but realised a market beta of 0.53, so it was never neutral; beta-scaled legs reached 0.560 at a realised beta of 0.232; and beta-orthogonal ranking reached 0.394 at a realised beta of 0.008. The pattern is monotone: the more market exposure is removed, the less is left. What remains in the neutral book earns +3.16% a year in 2015–2020 and −1.23% in 2021–2026, so it does not survive its own sample being extended. Shorting relieves the transfer coefficient; it does not supply the information coefficient that is actually missing.
Construction
Truncating a ranking at the top 30% was the worst sizing rule tested — discarding magnitude and two thirds of the diversification at once. It was nonetheless the default for every test in this project until the last day.
Capital
Data costs are fixed; returns scale with capital. A $150/month subscription adding a genuine 2% annually breaks even at $90,000 invested. Below that threshold, paid data buys precision in losing money — which is the constraint that most sharply separates this position from a fund's.
07

What the work produced

No tradeable signal. Two things of durable value instead.

A calibration procedure. Run zero-information signals through the identical pipeline and report a percentile. Every overlay — volatility targeting, inverse-vol weighting, truncation, band rebalancing — must sit inside that control, because each changes the return distribution independently of the signal. Conventional thresholds quoted without this calibration are meaningless: on this stack, noise reaches t = 2.75.

Infrastructure that is correct. A backtest engine tracking true holdings drift (algo/backtest2.py), metrics verified against empyrical, EDGAR retrieval with the pagination and primary-document traps solved (algo/edgar.py), 13F and fundamentals ingestion, an options collector running daily, and 35 regression tests pinning the specific bugs found (tests/). One constructs a zero-information volatility-timing sleeve — a sub-portfolio blended into a larger one — and asserts it still beats a naive beta-matched control — pinning the exact illusion that produced two of those overturned conclusions.

The literature cannot give you signals. It can stop you believing in ones you do not have — which, on this evidence, is the more valuable of the two.

Caveats. The universe is current index membership carried backward, so every result is survivorship-biased and should be read as an upper bound — and that bias is not a footnote here: measured in section 02, it is what produces the null floor itself. Fundamentals begin in 2012 at full coverage due to the XBRL phase-in. Accruals could not be tested at 16% cash-flow tag coverage. Spread estimators are biased upward in level; only their ratio is used.

Nothing here is investment advice. Figures are backtested research output on historical data, not achieved returns.
